# Phase 21 Session 1 — research and policy gate

Research date: 2026-10-04. The linked Catawiki Help Centre and policy pages describe **publicly observable rules**, not Catawiki internals. The schedule below is a snapshot; Catawiki says some lots may use different increments while it tests changes.

## Public Catawiki behavior

- [Next minimum bid](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/bidding-basics/how-is-the-next-minimum-bid-calculated): it is based on the current highest bid. The published euro bands and increments are €1–10/+€1, €11–100/+€5, €101–200/+€10, €201–500/+€20, €501–1,000/+€50, €1,001–2,000/+€100, €2,001–5,000/+€200, €5,001–10,000/+€500, €10,001–20,000/+€1,000, €20,001–50,000/+€2,000, €50,001–100,000/+€5,000, €100,001–200,000/+€10,000, €200,001–500,000/+€20,000, over €500,000/+€50,000. It says maximum bidding uses the same bands, while some lots differ during experiments.
- [Maximum bidding](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/max-bids/what-is-a-max-bid-and-how-does-it-work): automatic bids use the smallest increment without exceeding the private ceiling. Equal maxima favor the earlier bidder. A ceiling below the next normal increment can still be placed up to that ceiling. The ceiling is private; history does not identify automatic rows.
- [Reserve price](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/reserve-prices/i-d-like-to-place-a-bid-on-an-object-with-a-reserve-price-what-does-this-mean-and-how-do-i-know-what-the-reserve-price-is): a hidden minimum sale price; bids below it are accepted and shown, with an immediate below-reserve notice. A maximum below reserve is fully bid; a maximum above reserve initially matches reserve. [Seller guidance](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/estimates-and-reserve-prices/can-i-set-a-minimum-selling-price-reserve-price) confirms no sale if unmet. [Active reserve edits](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/during-auction-changes-to-removal-of-lots/can-i-edit-my-reserve-price) may lower/remove the reserve, subject to rules, but cannot add/increase it.
- [Fair business practice](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/eerlijkzakendoen) bars seller bidding on own lots and documents final-minute +90 second regular closing. [Closing help](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/bidding-basics/why-are-bidding-times-for-some-lots-occasionally-made-longer) specifies final 60 seconds +90 seconds.
- [Catawiki Live buyer policy](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/policies-guidelines/catawiki-live-buyer-policy): page and Live interface share one official bid flow; a valid bid in the last 15 seconds adds 10 seconds, including repeated qualifying bids. Stream/chat statements do not count as bids.
- [Live buyer help](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/bidding-on-catawiki/catawiki-live-for-buyers) describes account restrictions and registration details. [Auction restrictions](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/bidding-issues/why-can-t-i-bid-in-certain-auctions) include country/category rules. [Bid reservation help](https://www.catawiki.com/en/help/bidding-basics/what-is-a-bid-reservation) describes card holds based on account and activity signals.

## Hammerfall design and engineering inference

Hammerfall selects an explicit per-auction `fixed` or `stepped` increment policy. Existing rows remain `fixed`. The `stepped` policy uses the published table in integer cents, choosing the band from the **current visible price** inside the locked bid path; a manual offer must meet the resulting minimum. A proxy contest uses the band at the loser's newly visible price for the smallest counter, capped by the winner's ceiling. Partial ceiling bids and durable equal-ceiling priority remain possible. This is a Hammerfall rule based on documented behavior, not a claim about Catawiki's implementation. No hardcoded client decision may authorize a bid.

Reserve design for a later milestone: the private amount is fixed before scheduling in Hammerfall, even though Catawiki publicly permits qualified lowering/removal during an active auction. A manual below reserve remains accepted. Initial maximum below reserve becomes visible at its ceiling; a maximum at/above reserve opens at reserve. With competing maxima, the winner's visible amount is the smallest amount allowed by competition and reserve without exceeding its ceiling; ties keep existing durable priority. Closure below reserve has `winner_id = nil` while retaining the highest bidder as `current_leader_id`. Public state can say no reserve / not met / met, never the amount. The active reserve-edit race and post-auction offer flow are deferred. These are engineering choices, not observations of Catawiki internals.

Rapid closing design for a later milestone: persisted `regular` and `rapid` policies both use the same auction lock and PostgreSQL decision time. `regular` is final 60 seconds/+90 seconds; `rapid` is final 15 seconds/+10 seconds. This models only the deadline effect of Live, not video, chat, sequential presentation or payment.

Example reserve table for the later implementation, reserve €500/current €100 and no competing max: manual €200 → visible €200/not met; max €400 → visible €400/not met; max €700 → visible €500/met. If A has max €700 and B max €600, A remains leader and the visible amount is at least €650 under the €501–1,000 band, subject to the exact prior visible sequence. This last amount is a Hammerfall design inference requiring tests, not a published Catawiki example.

## Implementation and evidence

The first implemented slice is the opt-in stepped increment policy. Migration `20261004010000` assigns all existing rows `fixed` and protects the two allowed policy names with SQL. `BidIncrementPolicy` owns the euro table; `Auction#place_bid!` chooses a fresh manual minimum after the auction lock; `ProxyResolver` chooses a counter from the losing visible amount and caps it at the winner's ceiling. The presenter and transactional outbox publish the effective current increment through the existing v1 integer field. No private ceiling or policy name is added to public snapshots. Draft owners can choose the policy through the existing authenticated create/edit route. The required fixed increment input remains a compatibility field and has no stepped-pricing effect.

Focused result: 95 examples, 0 failures across new boundary/proxy tests, existing maximum and auction request tests, idempotency and independent-connection contention. Wider affected backend result: 264 examples, 0 failures, 1 existing opt-in live Kafka pending; includes bid, maximum, outbox, Redis projection, reconciliation and Kafka contract specs. RuboCop: 9 changed Ruby files, no offenses. A first wider run against the shared local Redis DB failed 14 projection/reconciliation examples because old keys with reused test auction IDs had greater or conflicting revisions. Repeating those 20 examples and the 264-example selection against empty isolated Redis DB 15 passed. This was local test-state contamination; it is not evidence of a new auction-policy failure. Logs are local at `/tmp/phase21-focused.log`, `/tmp/phase21-broad-focused-isolated.log`, `/tmp/phase21-projection-isolated.log`, and `/tmp/phase21-rubocop.log`.

Adversarial review of the increment slice: the server never accepts the browser's displayed minimum; manual and proxy choices use the auction row lock. Fixed rows retain their prior behavior. A rejected command with a retained key replays its historical minimum, and generated proxy amounts remain capped by the winner's instruction. The only new public value is the effective increment; the outbox still contains no ceiling, priority or policy configuration. The 5-run independent-connection race passed for both permissible lock orders. Limits: SQL bypass writers and mixed old/new application versions are outside this proof; enable stepped auctions only after migrating and draining old bid writers. The public event contract remains v1; live transport proof remains for Session 2.
